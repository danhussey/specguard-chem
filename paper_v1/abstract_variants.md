# Abstract Variants

## A. Conservative

SpecGuard-Chem is an oracle-compiled evaluation harness for agentic language models under chemically typed medicinal-chemistry specifications. The sgchem_v1.0 release contains 118 bundles, 656 tasks, and 266 test tasks compiled from machine-checkable witnesses and certificates. It evaluates construction, repair, candidate audit, rejection, abstention, verifier-tool use, boundary precision, representation invariance, and interrupt/resume behavior while isolating hidden oracle fields through PublicTaskView and deterministic validation.

The benchmark uses an ACCEPT/REJECT/ABSTAIN action space. A metric sanity pass shows why molecule acceptance is insufficient: deterministic local and verify-first baselines reach molecule_acceptance_rate=0.846, while the retrieval upper bound reaches 0.868. Their action accuracy remains 0.650, 0.650, and 0.673 respectively, with REJECT_recall=0.000, ABSTAIN_recall=0.000, and task_inconsistent_accept_rate=0.598. A deterministic verifier wrapper solves the test split, so the paper treats sgchem_v1.0 as an evaluation contract and audit methodology rather than a intrinsic chemistry-capability leaderboard. SpecGuard-Chem does not evaluate biological activity, toxicity, synthesis feasibility, therapeutic efficacy, clinical utility, target binding, dosing, disease relevance, or drug-discovery success.

## B. Balanced

We introduce SpecGuard-Chem, an oracle-compiled benchmark compiler and evaluation harness for chemically typed scientific-agent specification compliance. sgchem_v1.0 compiles 118 bundles and 656 tasks from machine-checkable witnesses and certificates, including 266 held-out test tasks covering construction, repair, candidate audit, rejection, abstention, verifier-tool use, boundary precision, representation invariance, and interrupt/resume behavior. The artifact includes bundle-aware splits, public/private task isolation, strict validation, prompt-leakage audits, oracle-scrambling audits, and negative controls.

Our results show that aggregate molecule acceptance can be misleading. Deterministic local and verify-first baselines reach molecule_acceptance_rate=0.846, while the retrieval upper bound reaches 0.868; action accuracy remains 0.650, 0.650, and 0.673, with REJECT_recall=0.000, ABSTAIN_recall=0.000, and task_inconsistent_accept_rate=0.598. A well-engineered verifier wrapper solves the full test split. Rather than treating this as a failed leaderboard, we use it to show why formalizable scientific-agent benchmarks should report verifier-wrapper saturation baselines before making model-capability claims. SpecGuard-Chem does not evaluate biological activity, toxicity, synthesis feasibility, therapeutic efficacy, clinical utility, target binding, dosing, disease relevance, or drug-discovery success.

## C. Ambitious But Safe

SpecGuard-Chem reframes chemically typed language-agent evaluation as an oracle-compiled contract problem. Rather than relying on template-filled prompts or hidden human labels, sgchem_v1.0 compiles 118 bundles and 656 tasks from machine-checkable witnesses and certificates, then evaluates agents through sanitized public task views. The 266-task test split covers specification construction, repair, candidate audit, rejection, abstention, verifier-tool use, boundary precision, representation invariance, and interrupt/resume behavior.

The release is accompanied by deterministic validation, negative controls, prompt-leakage and oracle-scrambling audits, clean-clone reproduction, Croissant metadata, and a claim ledger tying paper statements to evidence. Empirically, high molecule_acceptance_rate values for deterministic baselines mask action failures, while a deterministic verifier wrapper solves the full test split. We use this saturation result as evidence that oracle-compiled scientific-agent evaluations should be reported as specification-compliance contracts with explicit wrapper ceilings, not as unqualified model-capability leaderboards. SpecGuard-Chem does not evaluate biological activity, toxicity, synthesis feasibility, therapeutic efficacy, clinical utility, target binding, dosing, disease relevance, or drug-discovery success.

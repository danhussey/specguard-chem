# Abstract

Scientific language agents are increasingly evaluated on tasks that mix natural-language instructions, structured constraints, tool use, and abstention requirements. However, many such evaluations conflate model capability with prompt formatting, leakage, action-set ambiguity, and the availability of deterministic verifiers. We introduce SpecGuard-Chem, an oracle-compiled benchmark compiler and evaluation harness for chemically typed specification compliance.

SpecGuard-Chem emits bundle-stable train/dev/test splits, public task views, hidden oracle certificates, action-constrained outputs, and audit artifacts for construction, repair, candidate audit, rejection, abstention, verifier-tool use, boundary precision, representation invariance, and interrupt/resume behavior. The released sgchem_v1.0 artifact contains 118 bundles and 656 tasks, including 266 held-out test tasks, with strict validation, leakage checks, oracle scrambling, negative controls, reproducibility checks, and Croissant metadata.

Empirically, we find that molecule acceptance alone is misleading: deterministic local and verify-first baselines reach molecule_acceptance_rate=0.846, while the retrieval upper bound reaches 0.868, but their action accuracies remain 0.650, 0.650, and 0.673 with REJECT_recall=0.000, ABSTAIN_recall=0.000, and task_inconsistent_accept_rate=0.598. A deterministic engineered verifier wrapper solves the 266-task test split with action_accuracy=1.000 and task_inconsistent_accept_rate=0.000.

Rather than presenting sgchem_v1.0 as an intrinsic chemistry-capability leaderboard, we use this saturation result to argue that benchmark claims in formalizable scientific domains must report verifier-wrapper baselines and audit their public/private task boundary. SpecGuard-Chem is therefore an evaluation contract and audit methodology, not a measure of biological activity, synthesizability, toxicity, clinical utility, or drug-discovery success.
